## MODULE 1 INTRO — The Legal Foundation
**Approx. 90 seconds**

Before you write a single dispute letter, you need to understand the law behind it. That is what Module 1 is about.

The Fair Credit Reporting Act is the federal statute that governs everything in this business. It defines what bureaus are required to do, what furnishers are required to do, and what rights consumers have when information on their credit report is inaccurate, outdated, or unverifiable.

In this module you are going to learn what the law actually says. Not just a summary, but the specific sections that matter in a dispute. Section 1681e covers accuracy obligations. Section 1681i governs the investigation process. Section 1681s-2 holds data furnishers accountable. And Sections 1681n and 1681o establish civil liability when those rules are violated.

You will also learn how each of the three major credit bureaus operates, how their internal processes differ, and why the same dispute can produce different results at Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion.

Finally, we map out the full dispute timeline so you know exactly what to expect and when to escalate.

A strong legal foundation is what separates professionals from amateurs in this industry. By the end of this module you will know exactly where your authority comes from.

## MODULE 3 INTRO — Round 1 Precision Dispute
**Approx. 90 seconds**

Round one is where the dispute process begins — and how you approach it determines a lot about what happens next.

Most operators make the same mistake in round one: they dispute everything at once, using generic letters, without building a strategic case. That approach produces mixed results and trains the bureaus to expect low-quality challenges.

This module teaches precision dispute — targeted, substantiated, well-documented challenges that give bureaus and furnishers the least possible room to verify and return.

You will learn how to pull and audit a credit report correctly, identifying which items are inaccurate, outdated, unverifiable, or duplicated. You will learn how to categorize each item by dispute type — so you are not using the same language for a late payment dispute that you would use for an identity-based challenge.

We then walk through the AI-assisted letter drafting process. You will learn how to use the AI tools in this course to generate dispute letters that are precise, legally grounded, and customized to each item — not generic templates that bureaus have seen a thousand times.

Round one sets the tone for the entire engagement. Do it right and you give your client the best possible outcome.

## MODULE 4 INTRO — Bureau Responses and Round 2
**Approx. 90 seconds**

After your round one letters go out, the bureaus have thirty days to investigate and respond. When those responses come back, what you do next matters.

This module is about reading bureau responses correctly and building a round two strategy that escalates the pressure in the right places.

Bureau responses come in a few forms. Some items will be deleted — that is a win. Some will be verified — meaning the bureau claims the furnisher confirmed the account is accurate. Some will be updated — the bureau made a partial change. And some responses will be what we call a "stall" — a non-response or a procedural delay that needs to be challenged on its own terms.

You will learn how to read each type of response, what it tells you about the furnisher's level of engagement, and how to build your round two letter strategy accordingly. Round two is not just a repeat of round one. It is an escalation — more specific, more documented, and targeted at the furnisher directly, not just the bureau.

We also cover method of verification requests, which are a powerful tool for exposing inadequate investigations.

By the end of this module you will know exactly what to do with every response you receive.

## MODULE 5 INTRO — Goodwill and CFPB Escalation
**Approx. 90 seconds**

Not every negative item on a credit report is inaccurate. Some things happened. A late payment was real. A charge-off was real. That does not mean you cannot get them removed — it means you need a different approach.

This module covers two advanced tactics: goodwill requests and CFPB escalation.

A goodwill request is a direct appeal to a creditor or furnisher asking them to remove a negative mark as a gesture of goodwill — typically in cases where the client had an otherwise clean payment history and a legitimate reason for the negative event. Goodwill letters are not a legal tool. They are a relationship tool. We cover how to write them, when to deploy them, and what realistic results look like.

CFPB complaints are a different kind of leverage. Filing a complaint with the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau creates a formal record that the furnisher is required to respond to. When a furnisher knows a complaint is on file, the calculus around verification changes. We walk through how to file an effective CFPB complaint and how to use it as part of a broader escalation strategy.

These are the tools that experienced operators reach for when the standard dispute process stalls. Know when to use them and how.

We then cover tiered service packages. Most successful credit repair operators offer multiple tiers — a foundational tier for straightforward cases, a standard tier for more complex files, and a premium tier for clients who want full-service handling, faster turnaround, or additional services like credit coaching and monitoring.

We also cover your sales process — how to conduct a credit consultation, how to present your findings, and how to close a client who is genuinely a fit for your services without overpromising on results.

Finally, we address the referral engine. The most efficient way to grow a credit repair business is through strategic referral partnerships with mortgage brokers, car dealers, real estate agents, and financial advisors who encounter credit-challenged clients regularly.

This module turns your knowledge into a business model.
